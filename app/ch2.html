What does an LLM forget? · Chapter 2

Train the fact in reverse.

Task-vector negation measured a direction. Gradient ascent is blunter: take the model that knows Kyiv and climb the loss on that one answer, step by step. It works — but climbing a loss is a wrecking ball. Every number below is a real saved checkpoint, run live.

1 · Normal training descends the loss. GA reverses the arrow.

Training nudges weights down the loss L_f on the target answer. Gradient-ascent unlearning does the opposite — θ ← θ + η·∇L_f — deliberately making "Kyiv" less likely. The stabilized variant adds an anchor that pulls retained answers back toward the original model: J = −L_f + λ·L_retain + λ·KL(θ_full‖θ).

2 · The loss landscape

Every model is a point on a loss surface L(θ). Normal training rolled θ_full down into a minimum — where "Kyiv" is confidently correct. Gradient ascent runs the optimizer in reverse: it follows the gradient ∇L_f (the coral arrow) uphill, climbing the model back out of that basin. 6 · Why the anchor matters

Both variants suppress Kyiv by step 2. The difference is everything else: retain accuracy at each step, plain vs stabilized. The anchor trades a little forgetting speed for a lot of preserved utility.

7 · What this establishes — and what it does not

What it establishes

  • Ascending L_f reliably drives the target answer's probability down.
  • The retain/KL anchor measurably limits collateral damage at matched forgetting.
  • Forgetting and coherence are coupled: keep climbing and the whole model degrades.

What it does not

  • GA optimizes target loss — not equality to a retrained model.
  • A changed answer can be suppression, misdirection, or plain degradation — the entropy spike shows the difference.
  • These are discrete saved checkpoints; the "slider" snaps between real states, it does not interpolate.
